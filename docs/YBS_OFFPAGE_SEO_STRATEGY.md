# YBS Off-Page SEO and AI-Search Visibility Strategy

Baseline: October 2026. This is a plan. No external links, listings or mentions have been acquired, and nothing here should be reported to the client as done.

Principles (from Google's Search Central spam policies and AI optimization guidance):

- Links and mentions should come from real relevance, not purchase or exchange schemes.
- No scaled content, no doorway pages, no pages targeting competitor names.
- AI-powered search draws on the same fundamentals as classic search: useful original content, crawlability, and trustworthy entity information. There is no separate "AI ranking factor" to chase.

## B. Needs client approval or input

1. Founder biography, surname, career history and a headshot release. Named, verifiable authors are the strongest trust signal available to a small firm, but nothing can be invented.
2. A business address or service-area statement and a monitored contact channel, so entity information matches across the web.
3. Claims the client can substantiate publicly, for example number of members. None are used today.
4. Permission to publish the key membership terms in more detail (the Membership Agreement governs).

## C. External outreach (requires the client or an agreed outreach owner)

Target types, to be verified before contact. Names are candidates, not confirmed opportunities.

- Professional associations. Check the current member-directory and sponsorship options of bodies such as the International Yacht Brokers Association, the Yacht Brokers Association of America, the Florida Yacht Brokers Association and the Professional Yachtbrokers Association. Membership may be restricted by licensing, so eligibility must be confirmed first.
- Trade and industry publications. Candidates include Boat International, Superyacht Times, Soundings Trade Only and Yachting. Pitch only when there is a real story or expert comment, such as how brokers can structure an independent practice.
- Expert contributions. Offer bylined, non-promotional pieces on topics the team can speak to credibly: licensing and employing-broker arrangements, transaction coordination, KYC practices. Requires the founder's time and approval.
- Genuine partnerships. Marine attorneys, escrow providers, surveyors, insurers and marine finance firms that already work with YBS members may link to a partner page. Only list partners who agree in writing.
- Boat shows and events. Event pages and exhibitor listings often carry links. Costs are outside the membership and need client budget.
- Original resources. Resources planned on `/insights` (membership overview, launch checklist, research reports) could earn citations if they contain genuinely useful, accurate material. Do not publish them as thin lead magnets.

## D. Things not to do

- Buying links, link exchanges at scale, private blog networks, or paid guest-post farms.
- Competitor-name landing pages or comparison pages about named brands (Yachtr, YATCO, YachtWorld).
- Fabricated reviews, testimonials, awards or member counts.
- Mass-generated articles. Publish few, accurate, reviewed pieces.

## E. Measurement

Use tools the client actually has: Google Search Console (queries, indexing, rich-result reports), Bing Webmaster Tools, and server analytics. Keyword volume and difficulty data were not available during this research, so no figures are claimed. Re-run a manual query review quarterly and record dates and sources.

## F. Suggested order

1. Client supplies founder biography and profile URLs (unblocks entity and Person markup).
2. Verify Search Console and submit the sitemap.
3. Write and publish the first reviewed `/insights` article.
4. Confirm association eligibility, then pursue directory listings.
5. Approach one or two publications with a specific, expert-led angle.
